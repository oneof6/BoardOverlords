1 · What this is

This single HTML file replaces the physical Battletech Aces AI cards. You build one or many deck piles, assign them to AI-controlled units, deal cards each round, and the tool shows what each card tells the unit to do — in the original card layout and as a plain step-by-step checklist. It does not know your map, so you still measure ranges, check line of sight and move the miniatures yourself; the tool does the card handling, the ordering by priority, the bookkeeping and (optionally) the target maths.

There is also an alternative AI (tab “Flow AI”): instead of cards, a unit walks through a flow chart of decision points (“Am I crippled?”, “Can I kill something?”, “Roll a stance”, …). Use it per unit, or mix cards and flow in the same game.

2 · Quick start

  1. Open the Table tab. Under Options & presets load a scenario (Meet & Greet or Clash of Steel from the Aces rules) — or build your own: under Decks press + Add pile, under AI units press + Add AI unit and pick the pile for each unit.
  2. Tick Jump on every unit that has jump jets (a red J row becomes ground movement for units without them).
  3. In Round: if the AI team lost initiative, switch on AI lost initiative. Press Deal AI cards. Every card-driven unit gets a face-down card; only the priority number is visible, exactly like the card back.
  4. Press Activate next unit. The unit with the lowest priority number that has not moved yet is opened in the Active unit panel and its card is turned face up. Work through the movement rows (section 5), mark where you stopped, move the miniature, then press Done – next unit.
  5. When all AI units have moved (and the players have attacked) press Start combat phase. Use Next attacker to go through the cards in ascending priority and read the target icons (section 6).
  6. Press End round: cards go back into their piles, piles are reshuffled and the round counter goes up.
  7. Want to just draw a card? Press Draw on any pile — the card opens as a Free draw with the full interpretation, no unit needed.
  8. Press Export JSON any time to keep your setup; Import JSON loads it again.

3 · The Table tab

Round (top left)

Shows the four phases of an Aces round — Initiative, Movement, Combat, End — and the buttons that belong to the current phase. AI lost initiative controls the “If first” box: when it is on and no player unit has moved yet, an AI unit must use the “If first” instruction and ignore the numbered rows. With unequal numbers of units several AI units may move first: each active unit has its own Use “If first” tick-box that you can override.

Decks (piles)

Each pile is one shuffled deck. It starts as one of the four 8-card role decks (Brawler, Sniper, Skirmisher, Striker) or as a Custom pile where you tick exactly which of the 32 cards are in it. Several units can share a pile (the rules allow it for units with the same role). The big number is the draw-pile size; the line below shows draw / discard / in hand. Buttons: Draw (free draw), Shuffle, Edit (rename, change type or contents), ✕ (remove). If a pile runs dry it automatically reshuffles its discard; if every card is in hand, a fresh full deck is used and you get a notice.

AI units

One tile per AI-controlled unit: name, pile, AI mode (Cards or Flow), Jump tick-box, and the card slot (back with priority number → face-up → sideways once moved). Click a tile to select it; Draw/Return deals or returns a single card by hand; Activate opens it; Done marks the current phase finished for that unit (Movement or Combat). Flow-mode units have no priority number: “Activate next unit” takes them after all card units, in list order.

Active unit

The working area. The tabs Movement and Combat switch between the two halves of the card. During the movement phase the combat strip is covered (the rules say not to read it yet) — click peek if you want to look anyway. On the right you see the interpretation: the rows with automatic condition checks, your resolved move, and — in combat — the target priority chain plus the Target calculator. For Flow-mode units this area holds the flow runner instead.

Player units (roster)

Optional but powerful: list the enemy (= your) units with their stats and the tool resolves phrases like “toward highest health enemy” and the whole combat target chain for you. Fields: Alive, Moved (has this player unit moved this round — the AI prefers enemies that have moved), Valid target (in LOS, firing arc and weapon range of the active AI unit), Dist (inches to the active AI unit), Range band (S/M/L), TMM, MV, PV, Armor, Struct (current values), Pend (damage already marked this round — it reduces armor first, then structure), Threat (max damage it can inflict this turn) and Cover (the target has partial cover or benefits from woods against this attack). Health is calculated from armor + structure − pending. The Attacker stats boxes in the Active unit panel (damage at S/M/L and OV) allow the tool to evaluate “Can Destroy” and “Can Crit”.

4 · Reading a card

Each card has a priority number (shown on the back too), a Move Phase half and a Combat Phase half. The Move half starts with an “If first” box, followed by numbered rows. Every row begins with a movement-mode icon:

The Combat half is a left-to-right list of target icons. The leftmost is the top priority; every following icon only breaks ties. Hexagons are yes/no conditions, boxes with a triangle are stats (triangle up = highest, down = lowest):

“Use OV” beside the Combat Phase title: spend only as much Overheat as is needed to destroy the target if the attack hits (e.g. damage 3, OV 2, target has 4 structure left → spend 1 OV).

5 · Movement phase — step by step

  1. Order. AI units act in ascending priority (back-of-card number). “Activate next unit” does the comparison for you.
  2. If first? If the AI team lost initiative and no player unit has moved: use the “If first” box only. Otherwise ignore it.
  3. Read the rows in order. Row 1 is the main instruction; if it leaves several equally good options, row 2 is the tiebreaker, then row 3, and so on until one clear destination remains. The tool lists every row with a status badge:
    • true / false — a conditional row (“If no enemies within 16″ …”) was checked automatically from the roster; false rows are greyed out as “skip”. Anything the tool cannot know shows ? check with Yes / No buttons for you.
    • Blue chips such as → Warhammer — which roster unit the phrase “highest health enemy”, “nearest enemy” … points to (only enemies that already moved are considered; if none moved, all are).
    • board — needs a look at the map (woods/cover, LOS counts, “you can get behind”, elevation).
  4. Mark the last row you needed with Last row used. The movement mode of that row is the mode you use (Ground, Jump, Sprint or Stand still). Jump on a unit without jump jets is shown as Ground.
  5. Facing. The result box tells you whether to face the single enemy the instruction named or the majority of enemy units (if unclear: the highest-PV units).
  6. Move the miniature along the shortest path, then press Done – next unit. The card turns sideways.
AI Golden Rule: whenever an instruction leaves several options that the card does not decide, pick the option that benefits the AI unit most. Also: the AI wants LOS and range to at least one enemy that has already moved (so it can shoot), seeks partial cover — woods are preferred over partial cover — and always takes the shortest, most direct path.

6 · Combat phase — step by step

  1. The players resolve all their attacks first. Mark damage in the roster’s Pend column — the AI looks at the board as it currently is, including damage that is marked but not yet applied.
  2. Press Start combat phase, then Next attacker (ascending priority). The Combat strip is revealed.
  3. Each target icon is explained in order. With the roster filled in, the Target calculator shows a trace: which enemies each icon narrows down to, and the final target. If a tie remains, pick whichever target the AI would profit from most.
  4. Roll the attack as in normal Alpha Strike (someone rolls for the AI). Damage is applied in the End phase.

7 · Flow AI (alternative AI)

The flow AI trades the card randomness for a readable decision tree, plus one dice roll per move so the unit still surprises you. Switch a unit to AI mode: Flow in its tile and pick a role; its Active panel then shows a runner with a question and answer buttons. The Flow AI tab contains the full charts, a practice runner and the editable role parameters.

Movement flow

  1. Crippled? Armor+Structure at or below the role’s retreat threshold, or a crit that removed its main attack (always yes under Forced Withdrawal) → Break contact.
  2. AI moves first? → Opening position from the role (the flow equivalent of “If first”).
  3. Kill reachable? Can it get LOS and range to an enemy it can destroy (with OV)? → Finish it.
  4. Enemy inside panic range? (Snipers 12″, Skirmishers/Strikers 6″, Brawlers never) → Kite back to the role’s standoff range.
  5. Roll 1d6 → stance. The role’s two thresholds split the die into Aggressive, Balanced and Cautious.
    • Aggressive → Rush the role’s preferred target.
    • Balanced → already have LOS + range to a moved enemy and cover? Hold & fire; otherwise can cover be reached? Advance into cover, else Advance.
    • Cautious → can it stay at the standoff range with LOS to someone? Move to firing line; otherwise Fall back.

Combat flow

Sprinted/shut down? → no attack. Any valid target? → else no attack. Can an enemy be destroyed with normal damage → kill chain. With OV (OV > 0, heat ≤ 1) → kill chain + spend only the needed OV. Exposed enemy (no cover)? → exposed chain. Unit below half health? → self-preservation chain (highest threat first). Otherwise the role’s default chain. The resulting chain is a normal list of target icons and feeds the same Target calculator as the cards.

Editing the roles

On the Flow AI tab each role has editable numbers (standoff range, panic range, retreat %, stance thresholds), text (preferred target, opening position) and a target chain written as tokens: destroy crit nocover near health+ health- tmm+ tmm- range+ range- threat+ threat- mv+ mv- pv+ pv-. Changes are saved with Export JSON.

8 · Save, load, themes

  • Export JSON writes a file containing: all piles (including their current draw/discard order), AI units and their cards, the player roster, flow-AI parameters, round and phase, and your options. Import JSON (or drag the file onto the page) restores it; the file is checked first and an invalid file changes nothing.
  • The page also keeps an automatic copy in the browser (when the browser allows it). Reset wipes that and starts fresh.
  • Theme cycles Auto (follows your system) → Light → Dark. The choice is part of the exported file.

9 · Rules cheat sheet (from the Aces rules)

PhaseWhat happens
SetupGive each AI unit a deck matching its role; units with the same role can share a deck. Shuffle each deck.
InitiativeRoll initiative for the AI team, then deal one card per AI unit from its deck, face down.
MovementLowest priority number first. Flip, read only the Move half. “If first” if the AI moves before any player unit. Turn the card sideways when done.
CombatPlayers attack first. Then reveal the cards in ascending priority and resolve the target icons. Someone rolls for the AI.
EndApply damage and crits. Return the cards to their decks and shuffle.
  • Enemy units: prefer enemies that already moved; if none moved, consider all.
  • Woods/cover: always seek partial cover from the intended target; woods beat partial cover and are best when the target does not benefit from woods itself.
  • “Toward”: if the unit can end behind the target (outside its firing arc) within short range, it does so; otherwise shortest path, stopping just before base contact. “To X″”: reach that range by the shortest path, not by moving as far as possible.
  • LOS rows: if a unit has 0 damage at Long range, treat its LOS as extending only to the Medium limit (24″).
  • Jump rows → Ground if the unit cannot jump. Sprint and Stand still rows carry a condition to check.
  • Forced Withdrawal: use the first non-Stand-still mode on the card and move as close as possible to the deployment edge; otherwise face the most enemies.
  • Difficulty: easier = AI force ≈ 75 % of your PV and/or skill +1; harder = ≈ 125 % PV and/or skill −1.
  • Clash of Steel formation rule: AI units use the Battle Star’s Lucky ability whenever they need a 7+ and miss.

10 · Tips & limits

  • Use the optional movement dice (see the Alpha Strike Commander’s Edition) to show each unit’s TMM — then the roster’s TMM column is quick to fill.
  • You do not have to fill the roster: the tool then simply lists the rows and icons and you decide the targets yourself.
  • Distances and “valid target” flags are always relative to the active AI unit — update them when you switch units.
  • Ties are never resolved by the tool: the rules say to choose the option that benefits the AI most, so you decide.
  • The tool cannot see the map: LOS counts, cover, elevation and “you can get behind” are marked board for you to judge.
  • On a phone, rotate to landscape for the roster table; it scrolls sideways.
